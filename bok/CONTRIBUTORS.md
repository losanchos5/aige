## How to contribute

Contributors may correct or substantially revise a chapter or pattern and are credited by name. To
contribute:

1. Read `STYLEGUIDE.md`. Every chapter, 00 to 23, is drafted; contributors extend and sharpen them,
   add patterns as files under `bok/patterns/` (STYLEGUIDE §4), or propose new chapters from
   `OUTLINE.md`.
2. Write it to the template. Every factual claim needs a `[n]` citation tagged `primary`, `secondary`
   or `reported`, with a matching row in `sources/SOURCES.md` under that chapter's section.
3. Open a pull request. Add yourself to the **Contributors** list below in the same PR.
4. Keep the voice practitioner-direct, name categories rather than vendors, and never invent a figure.
   Respect the BRIEF blocklist recorded in `STYLEGUIDE.md` §7.

Substantive fixes (a corrected fact, a sharper argument, a better source) are also credited, in the
**Contributors** list.

## Roles and credit

Three roles touch the text. None of them changes the authorship stated above unless that is
expressly agreed in writing.

- **Author.** Writes and maintains the text, decides what is published in each release and answers
  for every claim in it. The author role is held as stated under **Authorship**.
- **Reviewer.** Reads a chapter or a dataset for a named version before it is released, against its
  sources and `STYLEGUIDE.md`: every claim supported by the source it cites, each source carrying
  the right verification tag (`primary`, `secondary` or `reported`), every "as of" date current,
  every mapping labelled illustrative, not a claim of conformity. A reviewer records findings as
  issues or pull-request review comments; the author records how each one was resolved. A reviewer
  discloses any commercial interest in a vendor, product or standard the reviewed text names.
- **Contributor.** Anyone whose accepted issue or pull request changes the substance of the text: a
  corrected fact, a new or better source, a new pattern, obligation row, glossary term or case.

How credit is given:

1. Contributors are listed under **Contributors** below, one line each: name as they wish to appear,
   an optional link, and what they contributed with the version it shipped in.
2. Reviewers are listed under **Reviewers** below once a review is complete, one line each: name,
   role or organisation (optional), what they reviewed and for which version, the date the review
   closed and any interest they disclosed. The line credits the review; it does not say the reviewer
   endorses every claim in the text.
3. Anyone may decline credit or ask to be listed by initials. Typo and formatting fixes are
   welcome and stay credited in the repository history rather than in these lists.
4. Signing the Thesis (below) is an endorsement, not a review, and is listed separately.
